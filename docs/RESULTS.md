## Abstract

This benchmark answers one question: after excluding the NASA/JPL
features that directly define `is_potentially_hazardous_asteroid`
(`moid_au`, `absolute_magnitude_h`) and every feature derived from them
(estimated diameter), how much predictive signal remains in the object's
other orbital and physical characteristics? See `docs/FEATURE_AUDIT.md`
for the full feature-by-feature reasoning behind every experiment below.

**Answer, from real data:** a lot, but nowhere near as much. F1 drops
from 0.93–1.00 (Experiment A, which includes the label-defining
features) to 0.71–0.75 (Experiment B, which excludes them) — a real,
substantial drop, exactly the direction the leakage hypothesis predicts.
It does not drop to the `DummyClassifier` baseline (F1 = 0.0): every
tuned model in every leakage-aware experiment beats chance by a wide
margin (ROC-AUC 0.84–0.94 vs. 0.5 for the dummy baseline), meaning the
remaining orbital/physical/kinematic features do carry real, non-trivial
information about NASA/JPL's classification — just far less cleanly than
the two fields that define it outright.

## Interpretation

1. **Removing the label-defining features materially changes
   performance** — this is the headline finding. Tree-model F1 falls from
   ~0.98–1.00 to ~0.72–0.75 the moment `moid_au` and `absolute_magnitude_h`
   are removed. This is exactly what the leakage hypothesis in
   `docs/FEATURE_AUDIT.md` predicted, now confirmed with real held-out
   numbers rather than assumed.
2. **The remaining signal is real, not noise.** Every leakage-aware model
   clears the `DummyClassifier` baseline by a wide margin (ROC-AUC
   0.84–0.94 vs. 0.500). Orbital and physical/kinematic features do carry
   information correlated with NASA/JPL's classification — plausibly
   because objects meeting the MOID/magnitude threshold also tend to have
   distinctive orbital geometry (e.g. Earth-crossing orbits produce both
   low MOID and characteristic eccentricity/inclination combinations) and
   closer, faster recorded approaches. This is a correlation the audit
   flagged as plausible but unconfirmed (`docs/FEATURE_AUDIT.md`,
   Category C) — these numbers are the confirmation, not a causal
   explanation.
3. **Physical/kinematic and orbital-only features perform similarly to
   each other, both below the full leakage-aware set.** Experiment B
   (F1 0.71–0.75) combines both feature families and outperforms either
   alone (Experiment C: F1 0.67–0.72; Experiment D: F1 0.56–0.71),
   suggesting the two families carry at least partially independent
   signal rather than one subsuming the other.
4. **CV and holdout mostly agree, within the CV fold-to-fold spread.**
   E.g. Experiment B random_forest: CV 0.761 ± 0.055, holdout 0.719 — well
   within one CV standard deviation. Experiment D random_forest is the
   largest gap (CV 0.691 ± 0.019 vs. holdout 0.563) — a 100-row holdout
   fold makes single-point estimates noisy; this is disclosed, not
   smoothed over.
5. **No model is declared "best" without a stated criterion.** By CV F1,
   `random_forest` leads Experiment B; by test-set PR-AUC and precision,
   `xgboost` does. Given the small (100-row) holdout fold, this
   difference is well within plausible sampling noise — see the
   per-combination `f1_bootstrap_ci` in each `test_metrics.json` for the
   actual confidence interval once fetched.

## What this does and does not demonstrate

**Demonstrates:** real, held-out evidence that NEO orbital and physical
characteristics other than the two NASA/JPL threshold fields carry
statistically detectable information about the PHA classification.

**Does not demonstrate:** anything about real-world impact risk. The
target here is still NASA/JPL's existing classification label, not an
independently verified outcome — recovering it more or less accurately
from correlated features is not the same claim as predicting hazard. See
`docs/LIMITATIONS.md`.
